Type 1: Research Project — Group 18

Healthcare Bias Mitigation: A Multi-Method Approach to Addressing Across Diverse Clinical Applications

Background & Context

Motivation

Core Clinical Challenge

Healthcare AI systems are frequently developed using datasets that are sampling bias and demographically imbalanced, leading to suboptimal or biased clinical predictions when deployed across diverse patient populations. Addressing bias across pre-processing, in-processing, and post-processing stages is essential for equitable healthcare delivery.

Our Research Vision

This project introduces a multi-method bias mitigation framework targeting key bias dimensions — missing data, class imbalance, outliers, feature scale differences, demographic disparities, record duplication, measurement noise, and domain shifts — across four distinct disease cohorts: Heart Disease, Diabetes, Respiratory, and Hypertension.

Scope of Investigation

Research Theme Covered

Theme 1: Data Pre-processing & Bias Reduction

Identifies and mitigates missingness, outliers, feature scale imbalance, demographic underrepresentation, record duplicate bias, noise, and domain shifts at the data layer.

Theme 2: Fairness-Aware & In-Processing Learning

Integrates clinical risk-aware constraint functions, adversarial domain alignment (DANN), and FairSMOTE to prevent systemic model disparities during training.

Theme 3: Multi-Cohort Benchmarking & Auditing

Evaluates performance across Heart, Diabetes, Respiratory, and Hypertension cohorts using statistical parity, MMD, Wasserstein distance, and class entropy metrics.

Prior Work Analysis

Literature Survey

Comprehensive review of recent research on medical AI bias, algorithmic fairness, and clinical data pre-processing.

Paper Title & Citation Core Focus / Objective Methodology / Approach Key Findings & Contributions Identified Gaps / Future Scope
FairBias: Mixed Noise Mitigation in Medical Imaging
IEEE TMI 2024		 To develop the FairBias framework that reduces bias in medical image diagnosis by handling mixed noise while maintaining diagnostic accuracy. Dual-branch denoising network with fairness-constrained adversarial learning. Achieved 14% improvement in equalized odds across demographic subgroups on chest X-ray datasets. Limited to 2D image modalities; does not address tabular or time-series clinical EHR data.
Evaluating Algorithmic Fairness in Clinical Prediction Models
JAMIA 2023		 Systematically evaluate bias mitigation at pre, in, and post-processing stages in healthcare ML. Comparative benchmark of SMOTE, Reweighting, Adversarial Debiasing, and Equalized Odds post-processing. Pre-processing techniques provided the best trade-off between fairness metrics and overall AUC-ROC. Evaluated on single-hospital datasets; multi-center domain shift was not analyzed.
Preventing Unfair Hospital AI Assistance
Lancet Digital Health 2023		 Address hospital AI bias to prevent unfair assistance and ensure equitable medical care. Subgroup disparity analysis and clinical risk-stratified reweighting. Demonstrated that standard accuracy metrics mask severe performance drops in underrepresented racial minorities. Does not incorporate automated outlier or duplicate record bias detection.
Fairness and Bias Mitigation in Healthcare LLMs
Nature Medicine 2024		 Evaluate bias mitigation in healthcare LLMs while balancing fairness and performance. Instruction tuning with fairness prompts and counterfactual evaluation. Reduced demographic stereotyping in clinical note summaries by 28%. High computational cost; evaluation focused primarily on text rather than structured lab values.
Ethical AI Design Principles in Clinical Decision Systems
BMJ Health 2023		 Identify historical medical bias in AI and advocate for ethical, fair design principles. Conceptual bias analysis, Ethical evaluation Develop bias-aware pipelines validated on multi-hospital and multi-demographic datasets. Provides qualitative guidelines but lacks quantitative algorithmic implementation.
Demographic Stereotypes in Clinician Pain Assessment
PAIN Journal 2022		 Test if perceived sex affects pain assessment and if gendered stereotypes explain biases. Controlled vignette study with demographic variation. Identified significant gender and racial disparities in emergency department pain medication ordering. Observational study design; algorithmic mitigation strategies were not tested.
Racial & Ethnic Biases in Cardiovascular AI Models
Circulation 2023		 Investigate racial and ethnic biases in AI-based cardiovascular disease models. Recalibration and optimal transport alignment across ethnic subgroups. Improved risk score calibration for Hispanic and Black patient cohorts by 19%. Targeted cardiovascular data only; generalizability to diabetes or respiratory cohorts unproven.
Algorithmic Resource Allocation Bias in Emergency Care
Science 2019		 Develop a framework analyzing how algorithmic bias affects resource allocation and response times. Health cost proxy analysis and commercial algorithm disparity audit. Revealed that using health costs as a proxy for healthcare needs introduced severe racial bias against Black patients. Focused on commercial cost prediction algorithm; does not cover clinical parameter pre-processing.
Gender Fairness in Chronic Disease Risk Prediction
Artificial Intelligence in Medicine 2023		 Check whether disease datasets and machine learning models are fair for males and females. Apply bias mitigation techniques across stages to reduce gender disparities. FairSMOTE and reweighting reduced male/female recall gap to under 2%. Did not address missing data or measurement noise bias simultaneous with gender bias.
Class Imbalance & Intersectional Bias in Diabetes Prediction
IEEE JBHI 2024		 Reduce class imbalance bias for reliable, interpretable diabetes prediction. Extend to demographic bias and integrate fairness-aware learning. Fuzzy SMOTE combined with feature scaling improved F1-score on imbalanced diabetes dataset by 15%. Single dataset study (NHANES); cross-domain generalizability not established.
Unresolved Technical Challenges

Research Gaps

Gap 1: Single-Stage vs. Multi-Stage Mitigation

Most bias mitigation techniques operate only at a single level (data or algorithm or output), and do not systematically address compound biases (e.g. simultaneous missingness, class imbalance, and demographic disparity) occurring together in clinical datasets.

Gap 2: Cross-Disease Pipeline Generalizability

Existing debiasing methods are typically evaluated on a single dataset or disease (e.g. MIMIC-IV cardiac data). There is a critical lack of frameworks validated across diverse clinical conditions such as Heart Disease, Diabetes, Respiratory, and Hypertension.

Core Project Goals

Objectives

1. Pre-Processing Data Debiasing

Design a healthcare AI framework incorporating equitable data collection and pre-processing techniques (GAIN, MICE, KNN Imputer, SMOTE, LOF, Wavelet, Quantile Transformation) to eliminate data-level biases before model training.

2. In-Processing & Domain Debiasing

Implement in-processing and post-processing bias mitigation strategies (DANN, FairSMOTE, Optimal Transport) within the learning pipeline to preserve fairness across heterogeneous patient subgroups.

3. Automated Auditing & Dashboarding

Incorporate clinical risk-aware learning and automated bias detection and auditing to minimize high-risk clinical errors and provide interactive visual inspection of bias mitigation results.

Formal Problem Definition

Problem Statement

To develop a clinically risk-aware, multi-stage healthcare bias mitigation framework that systematically detects, quantifies, and eliminates data-level and algorithmic biases across diverse clinical cohorts — ensuring equitable, accurate, and trustworthy AI decision support for Heart Disease, Diabetes, Respiratory, and Hypertension patients.

Empirical Benchmarking & Metrics

Latent Feature Extraction Methods & Metrics

Deep learning architectures and evaluation metrics used for cross-dataset feature alignment and latent representation benchmarking.

1. Deep Generalized CCA (DGCCA)

Learns joint non-linear latent representations across multi-view disease cohorts by maximizing canonical correlation across non-linearly transformed views.

2. SAINT (Self-Attention & Intersample)

Specialized tabular transformer utilizing self-attention over features and intersample attention over patient rows to capture complex cross-cohort dependencies.

3. Graph Neural Networks (GNN)

Constructs patient similarity graphs using clinical parameter topologies to propagate latent representations and preserve local manifold structure.
