Why This Matters

Motivation

Sampling Bias in Healthcare AI

Healthcare AI systems are frequently developed using datasets that are sampling bias and demographically skewed, which restricts fairness and equitable performance across diverse patient populations.

[1]

Accuracy Over Equity

Conventional machine learning models primarily optimize for overall accuracy, often neglecting performance disparities affecting underrepresented and vulnerable groups.

[2]

Identified Gaps

Research Gaps

RG1

Stage-Isolated Bias Handling

Most bias mitigation techniques operate only at a single level (data or algorithm or output), and do not analyze how bias propagates across the entire ML pipeline.

[1, 11, 5, 8, 15]
RG2

Lack of Clinical Risk Awareness

Fairness metrics optimize statistical parity but ignore medical consequences — some misclassifications are clinically more harmful than others.

[7, 14, 2, 9, 16]
RG3

Absence of Continuous Fairness Monitoring

Existing studies evaluate fairness offline on retrospective datasets and lack an integrated auditing mechanism that monitors demographic performance and detects fairness drift after deployment.

[11, 12, 15, 7]
RG4

Human–AI Interaction Neglect

Algorithmic fairness research rarely considers decision-level bias arising from clinician interpretation.

[1, 13, 9, 10, 6, 15]
Project Goals

Objectives

Overall Problem

Healthcare AI models trained on real-world clinical data often exhibit uneven predictive reliability across patient groups, resulting in unfair and unsafe clinical decision support. Existing solutions typically address these issues at isolated stages of the learning pipeline, limiting robustness for high-risk patient outcomes.

Design a healthcare AI framework that maintains consistent predictive performance across diverse patient populations to reduce unfair clinical decision outcomes.

Technical Core

Algorithms

cxr-foundation-bias Algorithm

Reduces bias by learning disease-focused features independent of sensitive attributes.

[3]
XGBoost (Extreme Gradient Boosting)

Improves accuracy by combining multiple decision trees to make reliable predictions.

[14]
Optimized Data Imputation Selection Algorithm

Selects the best imputation method to handle missing data while minimizing bias across different patient groups. (C-Chart Based DI Selection)

[9]
MICE

Fills missing values using relationships between variables to create balanced and less biased data.

[9]
Adversarial Debiasing (AD)

Trains the model to make accurate predictions while simultaneously preventing it from learning sensitive attributes (like gender or race).

[1]
CFReg (Counterfactual Fairness Regularization)

Ensures predictions remain the same even if sensitive attributes are hypothetically changed.

[2]
CEO (Calibrated Equalized Odds)

Adjusts final predictions to ensure equal error rates across different demographic groups.

[1]
Reject Option Classification (ROC)

Changes decisions near uncertain cases to favor fairness for disadvantaged groups.

[1]
QMix (Quality-Aware Learning)

Prioritizes high-risk patient cases during training to reduce critical misclassification errors.

[3]
Gate Recurrent Unit Network

Captures temporal patterns in patient data (like history over time) to improve accurate predictions.

[9]
FlipSet Algorithm

Identifies minimal changes in patient attributes that would flip the model's prediction, helping reveal hidden bias.

[2]
HAITI Computation Algorithm

Measures performance differences across demographic groups to detect unfair disparities.

[12]
Federated Averaging Algorithm (FedAvg)

Trains the model across multiple hospitals/devices without sharing raw patient data, preserving privacy.

[4]
DP-SGD (Differentially Private Stochastic Gradient Descent)

Adds controlled noise during training to protect sensitive patient information while learning.

[4]
Approach

Methodology

Domain-Specific Batch Normalization (DSBN)

Learns separate normalization statistics per domain, reducing distribution shift across patient groups. [3]

Domain-Invariant Feature Extraction

Forces the model to learn features independent of demographics, improving generalization and fairness. [3]

NearMiss

Balances imbalanced datasets by selecting informative samples from majority class, reducing bias in training data. [1]

Regularization

Adds fairness constraints/penalties during training to prevent biased model behavior. [1]

Treatment Equality Alignment

Ensures equal error trade-offs (FP vs FN) across groups for fair clinical decisions. [11]

Output Calibrations

Adjusts predicted probabilities to be equally reliable across different demographic groups. [11]

Focal Loss

Focuses learning on hard/high-risk misclassified cases, improving detection of critical conditions. [3]

Sample Reweighting with Declining Weight

Assigns higher importance to critical samples initially and stabilizes training over time. [3]

Theil Index (TI)

Measures inequality in model errors across groups to quantify bias. [1]

Average Odds Difference (AOD)

Evaluates disparity in false positive and false negative rates between groups. [1]

LIME

Provides local explanations to identify if predictions are influenced by biased features. [12]

Fairness-Aware Federated Learning (FAFL)

Trains models across institutions without sharing raw data while enforcing fairness. [12]

Secure Multi-Party Computation

Enables collaborative model training on encrypted data without exposing patient information. [12]

Health-FedNet

Combines federated learning and fairness mechanisms specifically tailored for healthcare data privacy and equity. [12]

Gradient Perturbation

Adds controlled noise to gradients during training to preserve privacy while preventing the model from overfitting to sensitive or biased patterns. [4]

Homomorphic Encryption

Allows computations to be performed directly on encrypted healthcare data, ensuring data privacy without compromising model training. [4]

Secure Aggregation

Aggregates model updates from multiple clients in encrypted form so that individual patient or hospital data remains confidential. [4]

Advanced Methods

Latent Feature Extraction

DGCCA

Deep Generalized Canonical Correlation Analysis

Learns a unified latent representation from multiple heterogeneous healthcare datasets by maximizing shared information across different domains, facilitating cross-dataset feature alignment and integration.

SAINT

Self-Attention and Intersample Transformer

Learns robust latent feature representations by modeling both feature-level and inter-sample dependencies using a self-attention mechanism, improving the representation quality of tabular healthcare data.

GNN

Graph Neural Network

Learns informative latent feature representations by modeling structural relationships among connected patients or features, capturing complex interactions that enhance predictive performance.
