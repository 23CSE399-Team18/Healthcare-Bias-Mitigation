=== PAGE 11 ===
Research Gaps
Stage-Isolated Bias Handling : Most bias mitigation techniques operate only at a
single level (data or algorithm or output), and do not analyze how bias propagates
across the entire ML pipeline. [1, 11, 5, 8, 15]
Lack of Clinical Risk Awareness : Fairness metrics optimize statistical parity but ignore
medical consequences are clinically more harmful than others. [7, 14, 2, 9, 16]
Absence of Continuous Fairness Monitoring : Existing studies evaluate fairness oﬄine
on retrospective datasets and lack an integrated auditing mechanism that monitors
demographic performance and detects fairness drift after deployment. [11, 12, 15, 7]
Human–AI Interaction Neglect : Algorithmic fairness research rarely considers
decision-level bias arising from clinician interpretation. [1, 13, 9, 10, 6, 15]

Objectives
Overall Problem
Healthcare AI models trained on real-world clinical data often exhibit uneven predictive
reliability across patient groups, resulting in unfair and unsafe clinical decision support.
Existing solutions typically address these issues at isolated stages of the learning pipeline,
limiting robustness for high-risk patient outcomes.
(objtective 1)Design a healthcare AI framework incorporating equitable data collection and pre-processing
techniques to reduce data-related bias and unfair clinical decision outcomes
(objtective 2)Implement in-processing and post-processing bias mitigation strategies within the learning pipeline to
improve fairness
(objtective 3)Incorporate clinical risk-aware learning and automated bias detection and auditing to minimize
high-risk misclassiﬁcation errors and identify performance disparities across patient demographics
(objtective 4)Integrate a privacy-preserving fairness mechanism that enables local (on-device) model operation to
support fairness-by-design without exposing patient data

Latent Feature Extraction Methods
DGCCA (Deep Generalized Canonical Correlation Analysis): DGCCA is employed to learn a uniﬁed
latent representation from multiple heterogeneous healthcare datasets by maximizing the shared
information across different domains, thereby facilitating cross-dataset feature alignment and
integration.
SAINT (Self-Attention and Intersample Transformer): SAINT is employed to learn robust latent
feature representations by modeling both feature-level and inter-sample dependencies using a
self-attention mechanism, thereby improving the representation quality of tabular healthcare data.
MV-DAE(Multi-View Denoising Autoencoder): is employed to learn robust and shared latent feature
representations from multiple heterogeneous healthcare datasets by jointly reconstructing different
data views while suppressing noise and view-speciﬁc variations, thereby improving cross-dataset
feature integration and representation robustness.
18 / 23


=== PAGE 19 ===
Latent Feature Extraction Metric
Cross-dataset alignment(CKA): Measures how well latent features from the four datasets are aligned
in a common space.Higher CKA indicates better cross-dataset alignment, making the method more
suitable for uniﬁed latent representation
Clinical information preservation(Macro-F1): Measures how well the latent features preserve useful
clinical information for classiﬁcation.Higher Macro-F1 indicates better preservation of clinical
information, making the method more effective for prediction
Latent structure(Silhouette Score): Measures how clearly clinical classes are separated in the learned
latent space.Higher Silhouette Score indicates better latent-space structure and clearer clinical class
separation.
Domain invariance(NMI and ARI): Measures how much dataset-speciﬁc information remains in the
common latent representation.Lower NMI and ARI indicate less dataset-speciﬁc information, making
the method more domain-invariant.
